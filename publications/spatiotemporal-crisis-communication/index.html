Can LLMs Reason Over Where and When? Benchmarking Spatiotemporal Synthesis in Crisis Response

Sawyer Alston, Yashaswini Sreenivasa Shetty, Sreeja Kanuganti, Anirban Saha Anik, Xiaoying Song, Lingzi Hong

A diagnostic evaluation of whether crisis-response models use information that matches a question’s location and time, not merely its keywords.

01 / MOTIVATION

Right answer, wrong location?

A crisis response can sound plausible while depending on evidence from another city or an outdated period.

In emergency communication, relevance is not only semantic. Answers also need evidence that matches where and when a situation occurred. This work studies how well models synthesize such context-sensitive evidence.

Research question

Do language models ground their answers in evidence that is spatially and temporally appropriate for the user’s crisis need?

02 / BENCHMARK

What the evaluation tests

  1. 01

    Crisis need

    A question that calls for relevant situational information.

  2. 02

    Semantic alignment

    Does the evidence actually address the requested issue?

  3. 03

    Spatial alignment

    Does it apply to the correct geographic area?

  4. 04

    Temporal alignment

    Is the information current and appropriate for the requested period?

  5. 05

    Grounded synthesis

    Can the model integrate compatible evidence into an actionable response?

03 / INTERPRETATION

Why the benchmark matters

By separating semantic from geographic and temporal alignment, this benchmark helps diagnose whether a fluent output is truly situationally appropriate. It connects retrieval quality directly to the practical information needs of crisis communication.
