Trustworthy language systems.

01Grounding & knowledge

Evidence-grounded language models

I connect language models to authoritative, up-to-date evidence through retrieval, knowledge graphs, and multi-agent systems. I study how to select the sources a claim needs, identify missing context, and produce answers that readers can verify.

  • Retrieval-Augmented Generation
  • Knowledge Graphs
  • Multi-Agent Systems
  • Evidence Attribution
Questions I study
  • How should multiple agents retrieve, synthesize, and critique evidence?
  • How can structured knowledge expose missing context and latent information needs?
  • Which evidence is sufficient to support a factual, traceable response?

02People & interaction

Adaptive human-AI interaction

I develop systems that adjust explanations to a reader’s literacy, knowledge, intent, and uncertainty. I study how to vary explanatory depth, ask focused questions, and give direct answers while preserving the underlying facts.

  • Controllable Generation
  • Health Literacy
  • Personalization
  • Proactive Clarification
Questions I study
  • How can one evidence base support explanations at different levels of detail?
  • When should an AI ask for clarification instead of answering immediately?
  • How can adaptation improve usefulness without changing factual meaning?

03Measurement & reliability

Evaluation for high-stakes NLP

I build benchmarks, metrics, and human studies to test whether AI responses are factual, consistent, useful, and appropriate for their context. Health and crisis communication reveal failures that fluent text can hide, including outdated evidence, mismatched detail, and information from the wrong place or time.

  • LLM Evaluation
  • Diagnostic Benchmarks
  • Human Evaluation
  • Crisis Informatics
Questions I study
  • Does a response use evidence from the right place and time?
  • Can automated judges measure factuality and audience alignment reliably?
  • Which metrics reflect what readers actually find understandable and useful?

A connected agenda

Ground the answer. Adapt the explanation. Test what matters.

These areas reinforce one another. Reliable evidence supports adaptation, human needs define meaningful evaluation, and rigorous evaluation shows where grounding and interaction methods must improve.
